INTRODUCTION
Chronic spontaneous urticaria (CSU) is more than a skin condition; it’s a 
chronic skin condition with an overwhelming impact on daily life. 
The Smallest Part of Life is an unbranded patient activation campaign 
created for the many CSU sufferers still searching for relief, empowering 
them to speak up about how their CSU impacts their sleep and daily life, 
and instilling hope for a life closer to their normal. 
Any materials developed as part of this campaign must undergo your country’s required regulatory  
review processes to ensure compliance with Novartis marketing policies, as well as any applicable local 
laws and regulations.
This implementation guide provides the support and understanding 
you need to create assets that effectively deliver the campaign in your 
market, so we can make a difference to people with CSU all around  
the world. 
After reading these guidelines, you should refer back to individual 
sections as required when developing your own assets.

01
CSU is a chronic skin condition that causes itchy and often painful 
hives, rashes, or swelling to appear on the skin, with symptoms 
potentially lasting months at a time.
Most common in women and those between the ages of 20 and 40, 
CSU affects around 1% of the population, and unlike allergy-related 
hives, has no clear external cause or trigger.
CSU impacts all aspects of life for the people that live with it, from 
everyday routines to important events. The physical symptoms, 
especially itch, can disrupt sleep, work, and relationships, while the 
unpredictability of outbreaks causes stress and anxiety, taking a 
significant emotional toll.
Misperceptions and low awareness around CSU severity, as well as 
difficulties in diagnosis, mean that many patients live in silence, with 
commonly prescribed medications such as antihistamines (AH) often 
failing to relieve their symptoms.

Objective:
Activate patients to speak up and effectively report their most 
significant symptoms and the impact of CSU on their daily lives. 
Empower them to have the meaningful conversation with their HCP  
so patients can ask for more. 
The value proposition for patients is: There is new hope for a life closer 
to your normal!
Strategic Objectives:
∙  Help patients recognize when their current treatment isn’t working, 
especially if they continue to experience symptoms while on AH
∙  Empower people with CSU to communicate the real impact of their 
condition on sleep and daily life to their doctor
∙  Give patients emotional permission and language to ask, “Is there 
something else we can try?”
∙  Prime patients for escalation moments by showing  
that a life closer to their normal is possible
Objective:
Increase CSU awareness, elevate discussions 
about the burden of urticaria  and drive 
patients to see their doctor.

public that it is a distinct, immune-driven 
condition, not just an allergy or hives
∙  Reframe outdated beliefs (e.g. “there’s 
nothing more they can do”)
∙  Build urgency by highlighting the 
unpredictability and emotional burden of 
the disease

Newly diagnosed and deeply affected, people with CSU often feel anxious, exhausted, and 
stuck. While they are open to finding relief, many struggle to navigate the limited treatment 
landscape, worry about side effects, or simply don’t feel equipped to speak up. So they stay 
quiet and continue to cope.
With renewed hope and permission to ask for better solutions, patients feel empowered to voice 
the full impact CSU has on their daily lives, from sleep to focus to emotional well-being. They 
recognize that other options may exist and take an active role in conversations with their doctor 
or dermatologist to seek a life closer to their normal.
FROM FEELING OVERWHELMED
TO CONFIDENTLY SPEAKING UP
∙  Patient language and escalation cues mirror the HCP message platform, focusing on itch, 
sleep, and impact on daily life (social & work)
∙  By expressing how CSU still affects sleep and daily life, patients trigger HCP decisions for 
treatment change
∙  CTAs build emotional readiness and support faster escalation decisions. The campaign 
discussion guide will function as our main CTA

action. It helps patients see that CSU is not just a skin condition;

and practical foundation for behavior change and treatment

MESSAGE HOUSE
THERE IS HOPE FOR A LIFE CLOSER TO YOUR NORMAL 
Tell your doctor about the impact of itch on your sleep and daily activities 
•  CSU has no known external cause and is not an allergy8
•  The condition can resolve over time; however, it is impossible to predict when6
•  The unpredictability of CSU outbreaks can cause stress and anxiety13
•  Many people with CSU avoid social situations because they are embarrassed by 
their visible symptoms, leading to social withdrawal and isolation15
•  The difficulties with concentration, productivity, and daily activities people with 
CSU face are similar to those faced by people with atopic dermatitis (AD) or 
psoriasis (PSO)5
•  3 in 5 people with CSU have difficulty sleeping2-3
•  CSU is a chronic skin condition with a big emotional and physical impact10-12
•  CSU and its symptoms are unpredictable, causing anxiety, 
disrupting life, and overwhelming patients3,4,13
•  CSU symptoms (especially itch) and their unpredictability impact all 
aspects of life, from everyday routines to important moments2
•   CSU is a chronic condition that needs ongoing treatment1,14
•   People with CSU have reported that AHs can lose efficacy over time and/or 
cause side effects such as drowsiness2
•  The majority of people with CSU are uncontrolled on standard-dose AHs1
•  Studies show that CSU symptoms are often viewed differently by doctors and 
patients; 1 in 4 patients report worse symptoms than their doctor is aware of7
•   More than half of people with CSU remain symptomatic despite taking 
antihistamines9,11
•   Your doctor may not realize how much CSU disrupts your life7
•   If your current treatment isn’t providing enough relief from CSU, it‘s time to 
speak up 
 •   Tell your doctor about your symptoms and discuss your CSU care plan

To show that it’s possible to minimize the disruptive, overwhelming impact of CSU on everyday life, we have 
developed an empowering, emotionally resonant creative concept.
The concept depicts a miniature version of a CSU sufferer, showing exaggerated symptoms, enclosed beneath 
an upside-down glass, illustrating how CSU symptoms can be reduced.
Alongside, their full-sized self appears relieved and confident, continuing daily life with milder symptoms. This is the 
aspirational goal for CSU patients.
The overarching message: With the right treatment, this condition can be reframed. CSU can become the 
smallest part of your life.

Photography is an important tool to 
communicate our message to the audience.
The imagery chosen should be warm and 
authentic. When using a glass element as a 
call-out, make sure the person is not obscured 
behind the glass, either by placing the glass 
overlay next to the subject or by placing the 
silhouetted, in-focus subject on top of the glass.

stock images, it’s recommended to prioritize 
photography that feels candid and natural, to 
reflect the authenticity of the campaign.
The imagery should feel positive and 
empowering to patients. It’s advised to avoid 
showing images of patients that are overly sad 
or depressed.

Our messaging approach supports patients as they move from silent suffering to 
confident self-advocacy, with renewed hope as the key driver of this change. It is 
designed to activate behavior by aligning emotional triggers, message focus, and 
calls to action across four key moments: 
1.  AWARENESS: Understanding CSU  
Recognizing CSU is not just a skin issue but also a chronic condition that 
impacts all aspects of life. 
2. CONSIDERATION: Ask for More Beyond AH 
Understanding AH may not be enough and that other options exist. 
3. ACTIVATION: Speak Up  
Feeling empowered to express how CSU impacts sleep, work, and daily life. 
4. MOTIVATION: Why It Matters  
Believing a life closer to normal is possible and worth asking for. 
Together, these stages build awareness, create urgency, drive action and 
sow motivation, ultimately guiding patients to explore new options with 
their doctor or dermatologist.

ABOUT LOCAL MARKET 
ADAPTATION
LOCAL MARKET ADAPTATION
Our goal is simple: to help people make CSU the smallest part of 
their life. We believe this is only possible when we adapt our global 
campaign materials for each local market.
This means keeping the campaign’s voice aspirational, while 
adjusting translations and respecting local cultural and regulatory 
needs. The central message, strategy, and fixed assets remain the 
same, but local teams have the flexibility to bring the campaign to life 
in a way that feels resonant for their audience.
By helping people with CSU recognize their ongoing burden and 
encouraging them to speak up, we can create the emotional 
and practical foundation for real behavior change and treatment 
escalation.
We recommend engaging with local PAGs to empower them as 
campaign ambassadors—before, during, and after the rollout.

TONALITY AND 
TRANSLATION 
The tonality of our campaign is aspirational, positive, and liberating—
this should be reflected in our campaign materials and in translations 
of our content. 
Headlines should always appear as statements, not questions, to 
present a clear and definitive call to action to patients.  
When translating this campaign into different languages, it is 
important to preserve the original intent of the headline and our body 
copy as much as possible.  
Content writing tips 
Keep our target patient segment, the Desperate Seeker, in mind 
when creating content. Think about the things they’d like to know, 
see and hear .
Content should feel friendly and supportive. We are always by their 
side in their struggle with CSU. 
Try to keep language simple and accessible, so patients of all literacy 
levels can easily understand our content.
